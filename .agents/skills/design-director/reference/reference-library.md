# Reference Library

Design exemplars and principles to draw from. Use these as mental references, not templates to copy.

## Stripe

**What to learn:** Clear hierarchy, precise spacing, trustworthy color, and conversion discipline.

**Signature moves:**
- Large, calm headlines with tight tracking
- Soft, palette-matched gradients used sparingly
- Code snippets and diagrams that explain complex ideas simply
- Consistent CTA treatment across long pages

**Apply when:** Building dashboards, payment flows, technical marketing pages, or anything that needs to feel reliable.

## Linear

**What to learn:** Dark confidence, motion as feedback, and extreme restraint.

**Signature moves:**
- Deep surfaces with subtle depth
- Keyboard-first, shortcut-aware interfaces
- Minimal color, maximum contrast
- Every pixel feels considered

**Apply when:** Building tools for professionals, developer products, or dense dashboards.

## Apple

**What to learn:** Product-first composition, generous whitespace, and material honesty.

**Signature moves:**
- Large product photography with minimal text
- Soft, even lighting and neutral backgrounds
- Human-centered copy
- Hardware and software feel integrated

**Apply when:** Building product pages, launch presentations, or anything where the object matters.

## Bauhaus

**What to learn:** Geometric clarity, functional form, and bold but limited color.

**Signature moves:**
- Primary shapes (circle, square, triangle) as structural elements
- Asymmetric but balanced layouts
- Red, yellow, blue, black, white, gray
- Form follows function

**Apply when:** Building bold editorial layouts, educational materials, or brand systems.

## Swiss / International Typographic Style

**What to learn:** Grid discipline, typography as image, and asymmetric balance.

**Signature moves:**
- Strong grid with flush-left, ragged-right text
- Sans-serif type at large scale
- Generous whitespace
- Objective, neutral tone

**Apply when:** Building reports, data-heavy pages, or any artifact that needs to feel authoritative and timeless.

## Dieter Rams / Braun

**What to learn:** Honest materials, quiet surfaces, and functional beauty.

**Signature moves:**
- Soft gray and white palette
- Thin lines and precise corners
- Typography as the primary visual system
- Everything unnecessary removed

**Apply when:** Building minimal interfaces, documentation, or anything that should feel enduring.

## General principles from the references

- **One strong idea per view.** All references share this.
- **Consistency over novelty.** A repeated element should look identical each time.
- **The grid is a tool, not a cage.** Break it intentionally, not carelessly.
- **Text is an image.** Treat headlines as graphic elements.
- **Restraint signals quality.** The best work often does less.
